Card 1 of 6

You buy, you borrow, tenants pay rent

You put part of the price down in cash and borrow the rest with a mortgage. Tenants' rent covers the running costs, the loan shrinks every month, and the home may rise in value.

It isn't risk-free: empty months, repairs, rate changes and market dips all happen. The stages ahead are there to catch them early.

Cash flow: what's left each month

Rent in, minus everything the home costs you: the mortgage payment, property taxes, insurance and upkeep. Positive means the home pays for itself. Negative means you top it up from your own pocket.

Card 3 of 6

Cap rate and ROI: two ways to judge a deal

Cap rate is the home's yearly income before the mortgage, divided by its price. It compares homes fairly, however they're paid for.

ROI (return on investment) is your yearly profit divided by the cash you actually put in, so it includes the effect of the loan.

Card 4 of 6

Equity: the part of the home you own

Equity is the home's value minus what you still owe. It grows two ways: every mortgage payment shrinks the loan, and the value may rise over time (appreciation). Appreciation is never guaranteed and varies a lot by area.

Card 5 of 6

Before you buy: checks and closing costs

Due diligence means checking the home before you commit: its condition, the title, what similar homes sold for, rental demand and the true running costs.

Closing costs are paid on top of the price when the sale completes: lender, legal, title and inspection fees. Plan for about 2–5% of the price.
